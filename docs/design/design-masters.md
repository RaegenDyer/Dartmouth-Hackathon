# 100 design references for visual exploration

Prepared October 30, 2026. This is a curated reference library for the Dartmouth
hackathon app, not a ranking of the world's best designers. The word *master*
describes an influential reference for this exercise; it is not a claim of
universal consensus. Each entry is one real individual, including people whose
practice crosses several disciplines.

The principles below are **our interpretations to explore in an original app**,
not quotations, instructions written by the designers, or claims that a designer
endorses this project. Borrow ways of thinking about hierarchy, materials,
affordances, color, and space. Do not reproduce existing logos, signature objects,
layouts, or complete visual identities. Use small, coherent reference clusters
for a direction rather than combining all 100 styles in one interface.

Product features remain mutable. Concept screens illustrate visual possibilities;
their labels, controls, and content are not a new feature specification.

## Three coherent image-generation directions

These are original concept directions assembled from selected principles above.
They are not requests to reproduce any one designer's exact visual signature.

| Direction | Reference cluster | Interpretation for this app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm editorial | Charles and Ray Eames (51-52), Alvar and Aino Aalto (76, 68), Dieter Rams (53), Susan Kare (42), Massimo and Lella Vignelli (6-7) | A calm editorial hierarchy, warm surfaces, readable text, generous spacing, and friendly sculpted icon metaphors. |
| Playful civic | Otl Aicher (38), Alexander Girard (20), Saul and Elaine Bass (2-3), Lance Wyman (41), Wim Crouwel (37), Margaret Calvert (39) | Confident wayfinding, a vivid but limited color system, clear actions, and tactile icons with a community-oriented character. |
| Evening room | Luis Barragán (79), Tadao Ando (80), Isamu Noguchi (75), Charlotte Perriand (78), Eileen Gray (77), Kenya Hara (81) | A quiet evening atmosphere, controlled pools of light, spacious composition, tactile objects, and strong text contrast. |

Across all directions, use **custom generated raster icon artwork** (PNG/WebP,
with transparency where appropriate) in response to the user's preference. Keep
icon silhouettes recognizable, lighting/materials consistent, and labels readable.
Do not substitute a stock SVG/vector icon library or code-drawn shapes for the
requested generated icon art. Visual assets still need small-size, contrast, and
interaction checks before implementation.

## Source notes and further study

This is a curated list informed by design knowledge and focused primary-source
research. The sources below substantiate selected people and reference clusters;
they are **not an individual fact-check or endorsement of all 100 entries**.
Discipline groupings are practical for this exercise, and the app takeaways are
our own interpretations. No biographical dates, current positions, or quotations
are asserted in the catalog.

- The [Design Museum designer directory](https://designmuseum.org/designers)
  provides institutional profiles across graphic design, furniture, and
  architecture, including the Eameses, Alvar Aalto, Wim Crouwel, Eileen Gray,
  Margaret Calvert, and Jock Kinneir.
- [Vitra's designer archive](https://www.vitra.com/en-us/product/designer) supports
  the object/material cluster, including Girard, the Eameses, Jongerius, Noguchi,
  Morrison, Grcic, and Yanagi.
- The [Vignelli Center at RIT](https://www.rit.edu/vignellicenter/) preserves Lella
  and Massimo Vignelli's design archive across disciplines.
- [AIGA's coverage of its 2018 medalists](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/this-year-aiga-awards-its-most-diverse-group-of-medalists-yet/)
  includes Susan Kare and situates her icon work within visual communication.
- [AIGA Austin's Cheryl D. Miller profile](https://austin.aiga.org/event-internal/an-evening-with-cheryl-d-miller/)
  supports the reference to inclusive graphic-design practice.
- The [Design Museum's Margaret Calvert exhibition](https://designmuseum.org/exhibitions/margaret-calvert-woman-at-work)
  documents the wayfinding and public-information reference cluster.
- [MoMA's Anni Albers collection](https://www.moma.org/artists/96-anni-albers)
  provides original-work examples for textile structure and material thinking.
- The [Pritzker Prize laureate archive](https://www.pritzkerprize.com/laureates)
  includes Barragán, Ando, Sejima, Nishizawa, Ban, Kéré, Doshi, Hadid, and Niemeyer.
- [Emigre's Zuzana Licko profile](https://www.emigre.com/Designer/ZuzanaLicko)
  offers a primary account of digital type experimentation.
- [MIT Press on Muriel Cooper](https://mitpress.mit.edu/muriel-cooper/)
  connects editorial design with interactive design research.
